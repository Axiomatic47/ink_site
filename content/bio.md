Joseph Kirchner is an independent researcher in legal history, political philosophy, and the separation of powers, and a property-management professional in the Twin Cities of Minnesota. He is the author of the papers collected on this site and of a forthcoming history of immunity built on a seventeenth-century Star Chamber file that had never before been transcribed.

Kirchner entered the University of Minnesota Duluth in 2009. In his first year he took fourth-year philosophy courses, and it was philosophy that held him. He left the university in 2011 after a family tragedy.

The credits from those courses later transferred to Normandale Community College, where he completed an Associate of Arts with an emphasis in philosophy in 2016. From that year he continued the research on his own, part time alongside his work, and since 2025 it has been his full-time occupation.

## Research

Between 2016 and 2020 the independent study was chiefly in linguistics, ethics, and social contract theory, with a focus on the Hobbesian and Kantian philosophies. From 2020, following the death of George Floyd, its scope widened to the law, with a special focus on qualified immunity as it is applied to police officials. At the beginning of 2026 that focus ripened into a complete genealogy of the doctrine, from *Floyd v. Barker* (1607) to *Trump v. United States* (2024); the recovery of its primary-source record, The National Archives file STAC 8/203/38, the surviving record of the Star Chamber proceedings that Sir Edward Coke reported as *Floyd v. Barker*; and the file's commissioned transcription by Christopher Whittick, MA FSA, former County Archivist of East Sussex, verified against the originals at Kew. In a declaration filed in the United States District Court for the District of Columbia, Whittick stated that, so far as he was aware, no transcription of the file had previously been made or published, and that its transcription, verification against the originals, and preparation for publication "constitute a serious scholarly undertaking." The genealogy is the subject of a book, *The Subject's Unanswered Plea: A Restorative and Comparative History of Immunity*. The leaf images, transcriptions, and working papers for the file, and for the second manuscript account of the case at Harvard Law School Library, are published on this site under Research.

Between 2023 and 2026 Kirchner also catalogued the discrepancies between the Hebrew, Septuagint, and Vulgate accounts of the Genesis narrative, which raise significant questions about the narrative's chronology and its implications for patriarchal structures. That reading, *A Restorative Reading of Genesis 1–3*, is planned for pre-publication on this site later this year. It will stand beside a second book, *The Holy Seed: The Fall of Babylon and the Making of Jewish Identity*, a history of how the community of returned exiles at Jerusalem, in the century after 539 BC, came to define its membership by descent, and of the three channels through which that idea might have reached it; it too is planned for release this year. The question both books put to their sources is the one set out in the Codified Democratic Order papers, published here.

Kirchner is a member of the Selden Society, the learned society for the history of English law.

## Work

Alongside the research Kirchner has worked in property management since 2019, and he has worked through each layer of the trade. He began in leasing and community coordination in the Twin Cities, then spent two years as a lease renewal specialist, running the market analyses behind renewal rate increases and securing renewal authorizations from third-party owners. From late 2022 the work was in HUD-assisted housing: Section 8 and tax-credit compliance, resident recertifications, HUD and city inspections, annual budgets, and delinquent-rent collection, first as a property manager in St. Paul, then as regional manager of a portfolio of twenty-four PRAC 202 and 811 properties, 525 units across five states, and finally as manager of more than two hundred units across Minnesota until that portfolio's sale in 2025. Across those roles he hired and trained on-site staff, wrote policy and procedure, and ran the portfolios in AppFolio, Yardi, and OneSite.

Since 2021 he has also owned Operations Consulting LLC, a small consultancy serving small businesses: daily operations for a bar and restaurant, a food truck, a salon, a warehouse, and a pizzeria, together with operational analysis, licensing, fundraising, payroll, hiring, training, business organization, and website construction. The two lines of work reinforce each other, and both reinforce the research. Compliance with a federal housing program is the close reading of a regulatory text against a live record. A portfolio budget or a licensing file is a document that must survive an inspector. The consultancy taught him to walk into an unfamiliar operation, find where it is actually failing, and repair the system rather than the symptom. The habits are the same ones the research runs on: read the primary record, state exactly what it says, and build the procedure that lets someone else check it.

Before any of this, in 2017 and 2018, he taught English and composed curriculum for primary and secondary students at the École Française in Hurghada, Egypt. The post was brief, and it remains one of the most fulfilling he has held.

## Character and Ethic

Kirchner brings a strong work ethic to complex, interdisciplinary problems, and he thinks and decides clearly in difficult, high-stakes situations. When the work requires that no stone be left unturned, he turns every stone. He holds a passion for history, culture, and language, and a firm belief in the quality of character their study builds. He holds it true that justice is a physical property of our world, subject only to the will of those who advocate for it.
