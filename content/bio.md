Joseph Kirchner is an independent researcher in legal history, political philosophy, and the separation of powers, and a property-management professional in the Twin Cities of Minnesota. He is the author of the papers collected on this site and of a forthcoming history of immunity built on a seventeenth-century Star Chamber file that had never before been transcribed.

## Education

Kirchner entered the University of Minnesota Duluth in 2009. In his first year he took fourth-year philosophy courses, and it was philosophy that held him. He left the university in 2011 after the death of his twin brother.

The credits from those courses later transferred to Normandale Community College, where he completed an Associate of Arts with an emphasis in philosophy in 2016. From that year he continued the work on his own: a programme of independent study in social contract theory, with particular attention to Hobbes and Kant, which widened over time into constitutional law, English legal history, and the palaeography of early modern legal hands. Since 2025 it has been his full-time occupation.

## Research

That cross-disciplinary study produced the work presented here. The Codified Democratic Order papers set out the Fundamental Laws of Supremacism and Egalitarianism and apply them to live constitutional questions. The Madisonian Separation of Powers Objective Compliance Test recovers Madison's own framework and derives from it a six-step test, checked against thirty-six Supreme Court decisions across a century and a half. The Abrahamic thesis carries the same method into the reading of Genesis.

The largest of these projects is a book, *A Subject's Unanswered Plea: A Restorative and Comparative History of Immunity*. Its primary subject is The National Archives file STAC 8/203/38, the surviving record of the 1607 Star Chamber proceedings that Sir Edward Coke reported as *Floyd v. Barker*, the case from which the doctrine of judicial immunity descends. In 2026 Kirchner retained Christopher Whittick, MA FSA, former County Archivist of East Sussex, to produce a verification transcription of the file against the originals at Kew. In a declaration filed in the United States District Court for the District of Columbia, Whittick stated that, so far as he was aware, no transcription of the file had previously been made or published, and that its transcription, verification against the originals, and preparation for publication "constitute a serious scholarly undertaking." The leaf images, transcriptions, and working papers for that file, and for the second manuscript account of the case at Harvard Law School Library, are published on this site under Research.

Kirchner is a member of the Selden Society, the learned society for the history of English law.

## Work

Alongside the research he has worked in property management since 2019: leasing, renewals, and site management in the Twin Cities, then regional management of a HUD-assisted portfolio of twenty-four properties across five states, and management of more than two hundred units across Minnesota until that portfolio's sale in 2025. He also owns a small consultancy serving small businesses.

The degree he did not finish is answered by the work itself. What the interruption left uncertified, the research now demonstrates, in the papers, the archive, and the book.
